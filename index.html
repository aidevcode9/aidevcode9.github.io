Process

Short engagements with clear outputs.

The goal is to give leadership a defensible view of risk, fit, and next actions without turning the engagement into its own bureaucracy.

1

Frame the decision

We define the exact decision being made, the operating context, and the downside if the wrong assumption survives.

2

Review the evidence

Architecture, controls, product claims, data realities, team constraints, and vendor narratives get compared against what is actually in place.

3

Rank the risk

Issues are translated into business language with severity, confidence, and the likely operating consequences of leaving them unresolved.

4

Give leadership a path

The output is a blunt read plus a pragmatic next-step plan: what to fix now, what to defer, and what should not move forward yet.

FAQ

Questions buyers usually ask before they reach out.

This site is intentionally direct because most engagements begin with a practical question, not with a vague interest in AI strategy.

When is the right time to bring in an external AI consultant?

The best time is before a large vendor commitment, before leadership greenlights a production push, or when a team needs an objective read that internal politics make harder to produce.

Do you only work on greenfield AI projects?

No. A large share of the work is reviewing systems that already exist, evaluating vendors that are already in the room, or stabilizing a roadmap that is already moving.

Can you support diligence-style reviews where AI claims need to be pressure-tested?

Yes. I can structure a technical assessment around architecture maturity, control gaps, team capability, and the likely effort required to make vendor or product claims durable.

What does a useful engagement output look like?

Usually an executive briefing, a risk-ranked scorecard, and a practical action plan with priorities. The output should help a leadership team act, not just admire analysis.
